Registration form fields
FieldLevelRequiredWhy it’s asked

Emergency contacts, T-shirt sizes and dietary needs are collected from finalists only, so the database holds less personal data. A hidden source field records which QR code or link brought each registration.

Data protection
  • India’s DPDP Rules were notified on 13 Nov 2025; the consent-notice and breach rules start 18 months later. Follow them anyway.
  • The notice lists the data collected, why, and how to withdraw consent as easily as it was given.
  • If data leaks, BRENSH tells affected people and Mindspace at once, with a full written report within 72 hours.
  • Access by role: the helpdesk sees contacts; judges see only act ID, act name and category.
  • BRENSH deletes all participant data 90 days after the Airoli East finale (by 13 Apr), except content covered by the release.

Ground Audition days

One category family per window

Each day has a lunch window (12:00–15:00) and an after-work window (17:00–20:00). Grouping by category means the right judges sit and the backline is set up once.

Bar length is minutes used out of the 180-minute window, at slot lengths of 5 (Solo Dance, Vocal), 6 (Stand-up), 7 (Group Dance) and 8 minutes (Instrumental). The plan is rebuilt from live counts on 4 Dec.

A day on the floor

Zone layout

One-way flow. Tap a zone.

Each act, step by step

  1. Arrives 20 minutes early; QR scan and ID check at the desk.
  2. Gets a sticker with the act ID; waits in the holding area.
  3. Stage manager confirms the track is cued, then calls the act to the wings two acts ahead.
  4. Host introduces them in 15 seconds: name, company, category.
  5. They perform facing a countdown screen. Amber light at 30 seconds left; music fades 15 seconds past the limit.
  6. No spoken feedback; judges write comments. This keeps the day on time.
  7. They leave by the opposite side; the thank-you message goes out automatically.

    The desk Judging and results

    Same panel, signed results

    Three judges score a whole category within a park. Five people sign the results. Finalists hear personally before anything goes public on 18 Dec.

    Try the rubric

    70out of 100 for one judge. An act’s score is the average of three.

    Rules that keep it fair

    • One panel per category per park. Book judges for every window of their category, or scores stop being comparable.
    • Calibrate first. All three score 3 sample clips; more than 15 points apart, they agree what a 5 and an 8 look like.
    • Conflicts. A judge who knows a performer or shares their company abstains; the other two are averaged.
    • No gaps. The tablet won’t move on until all three scores are in.
    • Ties at the cut-off. Higher first-criterion score, then the head judge re-watches the video.
    • Locked nightly. Scores go read-only at 20:30; every edit is logged.

    From scores to finalists

    1. Last windowThe system ranks every act in the category.
    2. ShortlistTop 8 per category per park.
    3. 16–17 DecReview call: judges re-watch places 4–10 and confirm 5 finalists and 2 reserves.
    4. Sign-offThree judges, BRENSH and the Mindspace approver sign the sheet.
    5. 18 Dec 10:00Personal messages to every act.
    6. 18 Dec 12:00Mindspace goes public, never before the personal messages.

    Appeals

    1. 48 hoursOpen until 20 Dec 12:00, by email only.
    2. Process onlyWrong track, timing fault, technical failure. Not judges’ taste.
    3. 24 hoursThe head judge and BRENSH re-watch the video and reply. The decision is final.
    4. Proven errorA video re-audition, scored by the same panel.
